Course project · Control theory

Quarter-car active suspension study

Advanced Control Theory course project, simulation only. It reproduces Na et al. (IEEE TSMC 2022) approximation-free active suspension control in MATLAB/Simulink: after fitting two plant parameters to the paper's Case-10 experiment, the unchanged controller lands within about 6% of its error indices (IAE 0.129 vs 0.122). It then tests the claims: the paper's Case-B gains fail a hold-out test, LQR and skyhook baselines match or beat it at equal effort, and an anti-windup extension keeps the wheel on the road over a 50 mm bump.

MATLAB · Simulink · C port timed for a Cortex-M4 · FreeCAD rig · browser lab

How this page works

Every ripple on this page is a solution of the 2D wave equation, ∂²u/∂t² = c²∇²u, stepped hundreds of times a second on your GPU with a leapfrog scheme. The name is metal: the waves reflect off its footprint, which is rendered from the same 3D mesh you see. Paragraphs sit on absorber, so the field goes quiet under the words. The three instruments run the same solver: time reversal in a closed box, a 1-bit reconfigurable surface choosing between two boundary conditions, and a lens made of slower medium.

Renderer: waiting for the GPU.

Built with three.js (WebGPU, with a WebGL 2 fallback). The letters are modelled in Blender by tools/blender/text_mesh.py; the surface tiles, posts and lens are built in three.js at runtime. Type: Archivo, Instrument Serif, IBM Plex Mono. Content from the main site, as of October 2026.
